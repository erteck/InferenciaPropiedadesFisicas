Consequence of not retraining on SDSS: the comparison E1 vs B0 measures the
change of images **together with** the differences between our pipeline and
Katachi's. To keep the second part small, the training recipe copies Katachi as
closely as possible (§3.4), and the notebook will state this limitation
explicitly. It will not attribute the whole difference to the change of survey.

## 1. Question the notebook answers

On the same Test galaxies and with the same Pipe3D labels, does a Katachi chain
trained on DESI DR10 grz flux images predict M\*, SFR and D4000 (and through them
t50 and the SFH) better or worse than the published Katachi chain trained on SDSS
gri RGB images? By how much, and with what statistical uncertainty? Do the SHAP
maps and radial gradients tell the same physical story?

**Endpoints, fixed before any training** (to avoid choosing the metric after seeing results):
- Primary: Test RMSE of log M\*, E1 (mean over the 3 seeds) vs B0, on the 969 Test galaxies.
- Secondary: RMSE of log SFR and D4000; σ_NMAD and bias of all three.
- Decision rule: E1 counts as **better/worse** if the 95% paired-bootstrap CI of ΔRMSE
  excludes 0, and as **equivalent** if that CI lies within ±0.02 dex for M\* and SFR and
  ±0.01 for D4000 (two one-sided tests). These margins are of the order of
  Katachi's noise-bootstrap scatter (0.023 dex for M\*) and of seed-to-seed variation.
  Otherwise the result is **inconclusive**. The margins can be changed in review, but
  not after Test is opened.

## 3. Experimental design

### 3.1 Sample and splits

- Catalog: `plan.csv` from the downloader (IDs, coordinates, FITS paths), opened
  read-only. Labels and the Train/Test split come from Katachi's `scalars.cat`; QC
  flags come from the Pipe3D DR17 VAC (`SDSS17Pipe3D_v3_1_1.fits`), downloaded from
  SDSS. The exact column name is **not yet verified** (phase 1); it must reproduce the EDA's
  QC counts. Images: `DESI_Candidatas_800_GRZ/descarga_22fb149046f162f3/fits/`.
- Cuts, as in Katachi: 8 < log M\* < 12, −4 < log SFR < 2, finite D4000. No QC
  exclusion. Every exclusion is reported per split.
- Splits: Katachi's own (Train 8,692 / Test 969 after the cuts; already verified by
  ID). Validation is carved from Train with a fixed seed and saved to a CSV. How it
  is used in the final run depends on decision D1 (§3.4). Katachi trained on 8,898
  galaxies. Of the 206 we lack, 196 fail the paper's cuts, 9 are the unexplained
  omissions above, and 1 has a D4000 value that Katachi's string test treated as valid
  and ours does not. This difference is documented.
- Comparison cohort: the 969 Test galaxies. Every metric
  for E1 and B0 is computed on exactly these galaxies (paired comparison).

### 3.2 Model

**E1**: the Katachi chain (3 ResNet50, ImageNet V1 initialization) trained on DESI
grz cubes. **First run: 1 seed (42), β=3 fixed, no β grid** (decision of 2026-10-03).
With one seed the verdict cannot separate the data effect from seed-to-seed variation;
seeds 7 and 13 are the next step. Planned: **3 seeds** (42, 7, 13). B0 is a single model, so the primary
comparison is **single model vs single model**: the per-seed metrics of E1 (mean ±
standard deviation) against B0. The ensemble of the 3 seeds is reported as a secondary
result, because averaging models lowers the error by itself and would favor E1 unfairly.

### 3.3 From FITS to network input

> **Revision 5 (team decision, 2026-10-03):** the network input is the central **300×300 px**
> crop at DESI's native scale (**78.6″, 0.262″/px, no resampling**), instead of Katachi's
> 50″ at 0.195″/px. Steps 1–2 below are replaced by "crop pixels 250:550". The crop is centred
> exactly on CRPIX. This adds a field-of-view difference to the comparison with B0, which the
> notebook states. Storage is float16 (max error in network input ≈1e-4, 40× finer than
> Katachi's 8-bit PNGs). All values live in the CONSTANTS block of `src/katachi_fits/config.py`.
>
> **Input variants:** `desi` (E1, no preprocessing) and `desi_prep` (E2, the team's
> preprocessing; a placeholder that stops with `NotImplementedError` until it is filled in).
> A variant receives the full 800 px cube and returns one of the same size; then the crop is
> cached in a folder named after the variant and a hash of its parameters. Cohort, splits,
> σ_b/s_b (always from raw DESI), recipe, seed and evaluation are shared, so E2 − E1 isolates
> the preprocessing. If preprocessing fails on a galaxy, its raw cube is used and flagged;
> if more than 5 % fail, the build stops. See `src/katachi_fits/variants.py`.

The transformation is **the same for every galaxy**, so absolute flux and color are
preserved. It mirrors what Katachi's input looked like: values roughly in [0, 1],
with zero flux mapped to 0 (black).

1. Central 191×191 px crop at 0.262″/px (50.04″, Katachi's field): pixels 305:496.
   The target sits at the center because `CRPIX=400.5`.
2. Bilinear resampling to 256 px (0.195″/px, Katachi's scale). Surface brightness per
   pixel is preserved, and total flux scales by the fixed area ratio (0.195/0.262)².
3. Stretch per band: `asinh(x / (β·σ_b))`, where σ_b is the median sky noise of the
   band. It is measured on full 800 px reads of 500 random Train galaxies (ring
   r > 300 px), after applying the same bilinear resampling as step 2, because
   interpolation lowers and correlates the noise. Default β=3 (Lupton et al. 1999).
   Zero flux stays at 0.
4. Linear scaling per band by one constant s_b (the 99.9th percentile of Train),
   **without subtracting the mean**. Zero flux remains 0, which is what Katachi's
   `ToTensor()` input had (black sky = 0). It also makes the rotation fill value of 0
   (§3.4) mean "no flux", exactly as it did for Katachi. Katachi did not apply
   `Normalize`, and we do not either.
5. Coverage flag: more than 5% exact zeros in the 50″ field in any band. These
   galaxies are kept, and their results are also reported separately.
6. No galactic extinction correction (Katachi did not apply one).

The 50″ crop is cached raw in float32 (4.2 GB; float16 would round faint pixels), so β can change without reading FITS again.
These six steps are the experimental variable ("FITS instead of PNG"). Everything
from §3.4 onwards copies Katachi.

**Decision D1 (needs your OK):** Katachi's scheduler looked at a held-out loss, and that
held-out set was its Test set. We cannot look at Test. The two faithful-as-possible options:

- **D1-a (recommended):** hold out ~10% of Train as Validation (stratified by mass,
  seed 42), drive the scheduler with the D4000 Validation loss, and train on the rest
  (~7,820 galaxies). This keeps the mechanism Katachi used (a held-out loss), at the cost
  of ~10% fewer training galaxies.
- **D1-b:** train on all 8,692 and drive the scheduler with the D4000 **training** loss.
  This keeps all the data but changes the signal that lowers the learning rate.

**Evaluation of E1:** predictions on images without augmentation. Katachi's Test
DataLoader also had the random transforms. Phase 3 checks whether the published
`pred_*` were made with or without augmentation, by running the published checkpoints
on `images.cat` and comparing against `pred_*`. If they were made with augmentation,
we also report E1 under that convention.

**Development step** (seed 42, before the final run): this is the only place where β is
chosen, from β ∈ {1, 3, 10}, by Validation RMSE of M\*. With one seed per β, differences
smaller than the seed noise are meaningless, so the rule is fixed in advance: **keep β=3
unless another value improves Validation M\* RMSE by more than 0.01 dex without
worsening SFR or D4000 by more than 0.01**. Test is not opened. With D1-a, the
development run for the chosen β **is** the final seed-42 run, so it is not repeated.

**Time and compute budget:** Katachi needed 133 to 206 epochs. With FP32 on an A100,
~1 to 1.5 min per epoch is a guess (the smoke test measures it), so 3.5 to 5 hours per
run. Runs needed with D1-a: 3 (β grid, one of which becomes seed 42) + 2 (seeds 7, 13)
+ 2 optional out-of-chain networks = 5 to 7 runs, about **18 to 35 A100 hours, or 100 to
190 compute units**. Colab Pro includes 100 units per month, so the budget may not be
enough. Options: buy extra units, use L4 (cheaper per hour but slower; the smoke test
compares cost per epoch), drop the out-of-chain runs, or keep β=3 without a grid. Training
checkpoints each epoch and can resume.

### 3.5 Training speed without changing Katachi's method

Rule: nothing listed in §3.4 changes. Only how fast each step runs changes, and each
change is checked to give the same computation.

| Change | Why it does not change the method | Check before use |
|---|---|---|
| Data resident in GPU memory (2.1 GB float16; the cast to float32 happens after reading) | Same pixels, same transforms, same order (shuffled with the run's seed). Katachi's DataLoader also used `num_workers=0`. | Pixels after the transform match the CPU reference on 100 galaxies. |
| Constant chain planes built with `expand` on the GPU, instead of numpy loops on the CPU | Same values | Equality test. |
| One `backward()` on the sum of the three losses instead of three calls | The graphs are disjoint (the chain inputs are detached), so the gradients are identical | Test: gradients equal to within 1e-6 for both versions on a batch. |
| `Adam(fused=True)` | Same Adam update in one kernel | Parameters after 20 steps equal to within float rounding. |
| No evaluation over Train each epoch | Katachi did not do one; its stopping rule uses the training loss accumulated during the epoch | — |
| Cache built by reading only the central rows of the FITS, in parallel threads, on a CPU runtime | Pixels bitwise identical (verified on the sample cube) | Bitwise check on 50 random files. |
| `channels_last` | Memory layout only | Outputs equal within float rounding. |
| `torch.compile` | Same operations, fused | Outputs equal within 1e-4 relative, and loss curves over 50 steps indistinguishable from eager; if not, it is not used. |
| `cudnn.benchmark=True` | Chooses among convolution algorithms that compute the same operation; results differ only by floating-point rounding. Katachi did not run deterministically either (PyTorch default). | — |
| Several jobs at once (seeds, β, out-of-chain networks) via separate Colab sessions or CUDA MPS | Each job is independent | MPS is tested in the smoke test. |

**Removed from the previous version** because they would change Katachi's method:
bf16/AMP (Katachi used FP32), 90° rotations (Katachi used arbitrary angles), and
per-channel standardization (Katachi did not normalize).

**GPU choice:** Colab (March 2026 prices, McCormick 2026): L4 1.7 units/h,
A100-40GB 5.4, A100-80GB 7.5, G4 (RTX PRO 6000) 8.7. The smoke test measures time
per epoch on each available GPU, and we choose by cost or by wall-clock time.

## 6. Why the notebook writes to Drive, and the guarantees

**Why it has to write:**
- **Cache**: reading 74 GB of FITS from Drive on every epoch is not feasible. The
  50″ crop of the 9,661 cubes takes ~4.2 GB (float32) and is built once.
- **Checkpoints**: a Colab Pro session can disconnect (inactivity or the time
  limit), and each seed takes hours. Without per-epoch checkpoints, a disconnect
  loses the whole run.
- **Predictions and metrics**: the evaluation, SHAP and SFH sections, and the later
  notebooks, read them without retraining. They are also the evidence for the
  numbers we report.

**Guarantees:**
1. Everything is written under one new folder,
   `DESI_Candidatas_800_GRZ/resultados_katachi_fits/<run_id>/`.
2. The code refuses to write any path inside the catalog folder or the `descarga_*/`
   FITS folder. It checks this with `Path.resolve()` before every write.
3. The catalog and the FITS files are opened read-only (`fits.open(…, mode='readonly')`).
   Nothing outside the results folder is ever overwritten or deleted. Inside a run's
   own folder, the only file that is replaced is the rolling checkpoint `last.pt`,
   written atomically (temporary file + `os.replace`), so a disconnect never leaves a
   corrupt checkpoint. Keeping one checkpoint per epoch is not possible: each holds three
   networks plus Adam state (~850 MB), times ~200 epochs.
4. The notebook computes the SHA-256 of `plan.csv` at the start and at the end and
   checks that it has not changed. It also checks the hash of 50 random FITS files
   against `inventario.csv`. (The hash in `configuracion.json` belongs to the input
   catalog of the downloader, not to `plan.csv`, so it cannot be used for this check.)
5. Drive space needed: cache ~2.1 GB, `images.cat` 1.9 GB, the other Katachi files
   ~0.5 GB, and ~0.9 GB per run (rolling checkpoint + best weights + predictions),
   about **10 GB** in total.

## 11. Implementation notes (revision 4)

Findings while implementing, each verified with code or a test:

| Topic | Finding |
|---|---|
| B0 predictions | `pred_*` in `scalars.cat` are reproduced (|Δ| ~1e-4) by running `images.cat` **without augmentation** through the published chain. With Katachi's test-time augmentation they differ by ~0.03 dex. E1 is therefore evaluated without augmentation. |
| B0 cohorts | Katachi's Test DataLoader has 986 galaxies (finite D4000); M\* RMSE 0.291. With the paper's cuts it is 969 galaxies, identical to our Test; M\* RMSE 0.226. |
| QC | Pipe3D VAC `GAL_PROPERTIES`, column `QCFLAG`. 139 duplicate mangaid resolved by exact label match (`log_Mass`, `log_SFR_Ha`, `D4000_Re_fit1`; distance 0 for all 9,661). Grouping 0=OK, 1–2=BAD, 3–7=WARNING reproduces the EDA (Test 918/50/1). Errors: `e_log_Mass`, `e_log_SFR_Ha`, `e_D4000_Re_fit`. |
| SHAP (audited) | `GradientExplainer(model, batch of 32)`, default `nsamples=200`; profile = channel mean of `gaussian_filter(map·1e4, σ=4)` then 1-px azimuthal average; ∇ = profile(20 kpc) − profile(0). **Katachi converts px→kpc with 0.5″/px, but its images are 0.195″/px**, so its kpc axis is stretched ×2.56. The notebook reports both conventions. Katachi explained image-only nets for SFR and D4000 (`sfr_solo`, `d4000`); E1 has no image-only nets, so its maps explain the full chain. B0 is computed both ways. |
| SHAP files | `shap_*.cat` are 14.8 GB each, so they are not downloaded; B0 maps are recomputed from the published checkpoints on `images.cat`. |
| Noise test | On B0 our implementation of Katachi's border-noise bootstrap gives 0.020 (M\*), 0.089 (SFR), 0.007 (D4000), 0.016 (log t50) on a 40-galaxy check, against the paper's 0.023, 0.096, 0.012, 0.021. t50 noise is in log10 units (Katachi's original network predicted log t50). |
| Scheduler signal | Katachi's held-out loader also had the random augmentation, so the D4000 Validation loss that drives the scheduler is computed on augmented Validation images. An unaugmented Validation RMSE is logged separately. |
| Final weights | Katachi saved the last epoch (no best-epoch restore); so does E1. |
| D1 | Implemented as D1-a by default (`DECISION_D1` in the notebook); D1-b is a one-line switch. **Still needs your confirmation.** |
